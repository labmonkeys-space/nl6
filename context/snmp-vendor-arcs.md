# SNMP vendor enterprise arcs: audits, PEN ownership, and what stays unverified



Extracted verbatim from `CLAUDE.md`, which exceeded the 150k-char auto-load limit. This is the long-form record: what the defect was, how it was found, and which mistake it is easy to reintroduce. `CLAUDE.md` carries the short invariants and points here.


**The three rules check ENCODABILITY, not SEMANTICS.** Nothing in them says an OID means what the MIB says it means: a value that encodes cleanly at its declared type passes even when the object at that OID is a different object entirely, or does not exist. The `palo_alto_pa3220` PAN subtree is the worked example — `1.3.6.1.4.1.25461.2.1.2.4.1.0` carries a number where `panMgmtPanoramaConnected` is a DisplayString, and `.4.1.3`/`.4.1.3.1` are not valid OIDs at all because they hang under a leaf scalar — and the whole table passed rules 1-3 (audited as nl6#569 and corrected by hand: 3 of 11 OIDs were right, 5 answered a value of the wrong KIND, 3 were not valid OIDs; the units reinterpretation nl6#541 made at `.4.1.0` was moot, since the object is a string either way). nl6#569 fixed that PROFILE, not the class: the `_comment` note is gone, `TestPaloAltoPANSubtreeMatchesTheMIB` pins the eight surviving values and the three absences as a RECORD OF A READING (nothing in CI compares nl6 against PAN-COMMON-MIB), the three real `panGPGWUtilization*` children were deliberately NOT invented in place of the invalid `.5.1.0` because their sub-identifiers were not resolved out of the MIB, and the other 28 profiles' vendor subtrees had no equivalent review. **nl6#590 audited the SECOND arc, Cisco's, and measured the same rate:** of 21 distinct Cisco OIDs shipped, 13 sit under `ciscoMgmt` and were resolved against CISCO-ENVMON-MIB (`201803210000Z`), CISCO-MEMORY-POOL-MIB (`201309180000Z`), CISCO-IMAGE-MIB (`9508150000Z`) and CISCO-SYSTEM-EXT-MIB (`201606140000Z`); **2 were right and 11 were wrong** (5 corrected, 6 deleted), against nl6#569's 8 of 11 — like for like, both counting distinct OIDs. At the observed miss rate an audit is weeks of work whose main output is deletions of data nothing reads, while a label is minutes per profile and is MORE HONEST THAN SILENCE: a reader who opens one of those files sees a vendor subtree and reasonably infers somebody checked it, and nobody did. The fourteen, one arc each: `check_point_15600` 2620, `dell_emc_unity` 1139, `dell_poweredge_r750` 674, `dlink_dgs3630` 171, `extreme_vsp4450` 1916, `fortinet_fortigate_600e` 12356, `hpe_proliant_dl380` 232, `huawei_ne8000` 2011, `ibm_power_s922` 2, `nec_ix3315` 119, `netapp_ontap` 789, `nokia_7750_sr12` 6527, `pure_storage_flasharray` 40482, `sonicwall_nsa6700` 8741. **THE MARKER IS PER PART, NOT PER PROFILE, AND 14 PROFILES MEANS 19 PARTS.** Five of them carry the arc in two parts and in each case the second part holds ONE vendor serial-number object among a page of standard rows; a per-profile marker leaves that file looking checked, and the label's whole value is that a reader who opens the file sees it. **THE MARKER IS A SENTINEL CARRYING THE PEN, NOT PROSE.** Prose matching fails on a rewording and passes on a label that says nothing, and a PEN-less marker would let a profile that later gains a SECOND arc inherit the first one's label silently, so a labelled 2620 does not excuse an unlabelled 1234 in the same file. `git grep UNAUDITED-ARC` finds every one. Each label states five things and nothing else: the objects under the PEN have not been read against any of the vendor's MIBs; the PEN itself IS checked by `TestEveryProfileServesOnlyItsOwnVendorArc` (nl6#588, PR nl6#589), so what is unverified is what sits BELOW the number; what unverified covers (defined at all, value of the kind its SYNTAX declares, readable at all); that nothing in this repo reads the arc; and the audited miss rates so a reader can calibrate (8 of 11, 11 of 13, 6 of 6, 0 of 1, 13 of 15 distinct OIDs). **No per-vendor detail beyond that is written down, deliberately**: an obtainability survey for all nineteen vendors exists in nl6#590, but the label's value is that it claims only what is known about THIS data. **THREE EXCLUSIONS, EACH FOR ITS OWN REASON:** `aws_s3_storage` / 32473 is RFC 5612's documentation PEN, chosen deliberately by nl6#588 and already documented, so there is no vendor MIB to audit against and no vendor claim to label; PEN 0 on `cisco_catalyst_9500`, `cisco_nexus_9500`, `juniper_mx960` and `palo_alto_pa3220` is the `1.3.6.1.4.1.0.0` `entPhysicalVendorType` placeholder (208 responses), IANA-reserved and not a vendor subtree; and NVIDIA's 5703 sits in the AUDITED set rather than the labelled one because its existing `_comment` says something STRONGER than a label would (no NVIDIA SNMP GPU MIB exists, so every object below the PEN is nl6's own invention, and `UNAUDITED-ARC` would understate what is known). **THE FOUR PEN-0 ROWS CARRY `scanVisible: false` AND IT IS CHECKED BOTH WAYS.** `oidTypeTable` does not type `entPhysicalVendorType`, so the production predicate the scan shares with the wire reads those responses as OCTET STRINGs and never reports them; they are covered by `assertEntPhysicalVendorTypeIsNotCrossVendor` instead. If `oidTypeTable` gains that row the four flip to visible, `TestUnauditedArcRegistriesAreCurated` fails, and the disposition is decided again rather than inherited. A stale exclusion that describes nothing fails the same test. **THE POSITIVE CONTROL HAS TWO ARMS AND BOTH ARE NEEDED:** a synthetic profile serving an UNLABELLED arc must be reported, and a second one serving a LABELLED arc must NOT be, because a rule that reported every arc it saw passes the first arm alone. `TestNoStaleUnauditedArcLabel` is the mirror of the guard (every arc is accounted for / every account describes something): a marker naming a PEN its own part serves no entry under is reported, and so is a marker on an arc that has since been AUDITED, since after an audit the label tells a reader the opposite of the truth. **WHAT NO TEST HERE CAN DO IS CHECK THAT A LABEL IS TRUE** (a label claims nobody read a MIB, and no test can read a MIB); what it refuses is the fourth state, a vendor subtree that reads as checked because nothing says otherwise. **NO OID DATA CHANGED AND NO GOLDEN DIGEST MOVED**: `_comment` is a top-level key the production decoder ignores and `shippedSNMPEntries` reads `doc.SNMP` only, so there is NO ledger and NO `newestFirstReversals` entry, and the guard's file is deliberately NOT named `*_ledger_test.go` (nl6#600's registry requires every ledger file to contribute exactly one reversal). Same disposition as nl6#601. **TWO CAVEATS STAND:** absence of a consumer IN THIS REPO is not absence in the world (`pollaris.mdx` is simply the only contract nl6 publishes, so an operator keying on one of the fourteen moves that arc up), and the four consumed arcs are consumed by nl6 EMITTING those OIDs in a trap rather than by a collector keying on them, which is what makes cross-surface disagreement possible there and nowhere else.

**NO nl6 RULE MODELS MAX-ACCESS (nl6#591).** The load rules check ENCODABILITY, the PEN guards check vendor IDENTITY, and nl6#590's reading tests check names, types and values — none of them models ACCESS, and none of them can, because an access mode is a property of the MIB and nl6 has no MIB. `1.3.6.1.4.1.9.2.1.54.0` is `writeMem` in OLD-CISCO-SYSTEM-MIB, `ACCESS write-only`, and `cisco_catalyst_9500` (`393084300`) and `cisco_crs_x` (`1451548800`) both answered it. **This is a different CLASS, not one more wrong value:** the object exists, the arc is right, the instance sub-identifier is right, and the value encoded as the declared INTEGER — that is WHY every guard passed it. It is also a COMMAND rather than a datum (writing to it saves the running configuration, writing `0` erases config memory), so a collector that reads it as an integer has learned something false in a way worse than a wrong number. **DELETED, not corrected** — a write-only object has no correct readable value — with `TestCiscoWriteOnlyObjectsAreAbsent` pinning both absences, extending across every Cisco profile to `netConfigSet`/`hostConfigSet`/`writeNet` (none ship), and pinning the deliberately-untouched neighbours (`.56`, `.58`, `.8`) as PRESENCES so the scope boundary is a measurement. **CITED BY NAME AND FORM, NOT REVISION, and that is not pedantry:** OLD-CISCO-SYSTEM-MIB is SMIv1 (imports `OBJECT-TYPE` from RFC-1212, spells `ACCESS` not `MAX-ACCESS`), so it carries no `MODULE-IDENTITY` and there is no `LAST-UPDATED` to quote — the only date is its header, `Copyright (c) 1994-1995 by cisco Systems, Inc.` The arc was RESOLVED, not assumed: CISCO-SMI (`201601150000Z`) puts `cisco` at `{ enterprises 9 }` and `local` at `{ cisco 2 }`, and OLD-CISCO-SYSTEM-MIB puts `lsystem` at `{ local 1 }`, `writeMem` at `{ lsystem 54 }`. No MIB file is checked in, same licensing decision as nl6#590. **`not-accessible` IS THE LARGER, UNSWEPT HALF:** every SMIv2 table INDEX column is `not-accessible`, so a profile shipping an index column as a readable row makes the same mistake in the commonest possible place. It cannot be swept generically — deciding it needs the arc's MIB — so the class advances WITH nl6#590's per-arc audit rather than being closed here. Both corpus digests moved (measured, not predicted: two triples left `shippedTagDigest`, and the OID NAME left the corpus entirely, unlike nl6#590 where two of seven deleted names survived), reversed by `snmp_shipped_cisco_writeonly_ledger_test.go`; nl6#590's Arista arc has since taken over as the newest link. **THE CHAIN IS STATED ONCE**, in the Arista paragraph — do not restate it here.

**A WRONG-OWNER ENTERPRISE ARC, and what nl6#576 actually closed.** The three `nvidia_*` profiles served GPU telemetry under `1.3.6.1.4.1.53246`, which IANA allocates to **Mailteck, S.A.**, an unrelated Spanish company — so a collector doing vendor detection on a simulated DGX resolved it as Mailteck. The whole arc moved to `1.3.6.1.4.1.5703`, NVIDIA Corporation's real PEN, preserving **every sub-identifier below the PEN**, so a downstream pollaris rule needs only its prefix updated. 225 rows: 222 static OID keys (74 per profile) plus the three per-model `sysObjectID` **values** `…1.2.{1,2,3}`, which stay distinct and in order. The live half moved too: `metrics_oids.go`'s `nvidiaGPUMetricPrefix` is the ONE constant driving 64 dynamic OIDs per device, and re-homing the JSON while leaving it behind is the mutation `TestEveryNvidiaGPUOIDIsAnsweredAtTheNewArc` exists to catch (it spells the new arc as a LITERAL for exactly that reason). **Scope: this is the NVIDIA instance, not the class.** The per-profile own-vendor-enterprise-OID guard the issue proposed was deliberately NOT built there — it needs an allowlist decision and would have failed CI against the pre-re-home corpus — so what ships from nl6#576 is a single-arc absence guard, `TestNoNvidiaOIDsShipUnderMailteck`. **nl6#588 built the general guard; the paragraph after this one is the current state.** **That guard reads OID-typed VALUES as well as names**, and the value half is the one that matters: vendor detection reads the `sysObjectID.0` response, not an OID key, so a name-only scan is blind to the exact regression it exists to catch (planting `53246.1.2.9` as `cisco_ios`'s `sysObjectID` left an earlier cut green while five golden digests failed, whose documented remedy is to re-pin — the nl6#541 shape). Its positive control therefore plants TWO rows, one in each position. `TestArcRewriteSeesEveryShippedSpelling` covers the trap-catalog surface and the alternative spellings that `shippedSNMPEntries` cannot see, because the ledger's un-rename keys on one exact prefix string. **The premise is checked in as data**, per nl6#541: `testdata/iana/enterprise_numbers.tsv` carries both registry rows with the source URL, fetch date and the command that produced them, and `TestArcPrefixesMatchTheIANARegistry` compares the arc constants against it — it was the only claim in the change that nothing else could falsify. **Both corpus digests moved and both are re-derived, not accepted:** `shippedTagDigest` because it is keyed on the OID string, and `shippedOIDEncodingDigest` because it hashes distinct OID NAMES (and OID-typed values) against their encodings. `snmp_shipped_nvidia_arc_ledger_test.go` reverses this rename against the corpus at 1bca8e8; several newer links have landed since, and every reversal walks back along all of them. **THE CHAIN IS STATED ONCE**, in the Arista paragraph above (the newest link) — it was repeated in four paragraphs and three of them went stale, so do not restate it here. **The residual, recorded rather than fixed:** NVIDIA publishes no SNMP GPU MIB at all, so the objects below the PEN remain nl6's invention — unattributed under 53246, attributed to NVIDIA under 5703. That is nl6#569's class narrowed to the object layer, accepted deliberately, and it is why the six `_comment`s and the doc admonitions are part of the change rather than optional polish.

**EVERY PROFILE SERVES ITS OWN VENDOR'S PEN AND NO OTHER (nl6#588).** A vendor enterprise subtree is an IDENTITY CLAIM, not an approximation, and four instances of the same defect shipped before this closed the class: nl6#569's twelve non-Palo-Alto profiles serving the PAN subtree, nl6#576's three `nvidia_*` profiles under Mailteck's 53246, the bare Juniper `jnxOperatingCPU` column four Cisco profiles carried (deleted by nl6#571 for a different reason), and the one this change fixes. **The data fix is one row:** `aws_s3_storage` answered `sysObjectID.0` with `1.3.6.1.4.1.9999`, which IANA allocates to **Zerna, Koepper & Partner**, a German engineering firm unrelated to Amazon or to storage. It now answers `1.3.6.1.4.1.32473.1.1` — **RFC 5612's documentation PEN, held by IANA, chosen over Amazon's real numbers (4843, 60099) deliberately**: the profile models a CATEGORY (an S3-compatible object storage gateway, which MinIO and Ceph RGW also implement) and AWS's own S3 is an HTTP service with no SNMP surface, so naming Amazon would trade one misattribution for a more plausible one. The cost is accepted: vendor detection resolves this profile to nobody. **32473 is a PER-PROFILE allowance, never globally permitted** — making it general would give any future profile a way to dodge the guard — and `TestOwnVendorPENMapIsCuratedAndComplete` pins that exactly one profile claims it. **Only `shippedOIDEncodingDigest` moved, and that was MEASURED:** the OID key and its tag are unchanged, so `shippedTagDigest` did not, which makes nl6#588 the first link in the chain to move one digest rather than both; newer links have taken over since (nl6#590's Cisco arc, then nl6#591, then nl6#590's Arista arc) and every older reversal begins by walking back along them. **THE CHAIN IS STATED ONCE**, in the Arista paragraph — do not restate it here. **Four things in `TestEveryProfileServesOnlyItsOwnVendorArc` are load-bearing, each a defect already shipped once.** (1) It reads OID-typed VALUES as well as names, through `scanArcPositions` — the ONE scan `entriesTouchingArc` was split into, so narrowing it to names fails both guards' controls at once; a name-only scan is what hid this defect from nl6#587's own research census. (2) Its positive control plants ACROSS profiles (a Juniper name in `cisco_ios`, an NVIDIA `sysObjectID` VALUE in `linux_server`, a Huawei name in `ibm_power_s922`) and is scoped to the planted profiles rather than the corpus total, so it stays trustworthy while a real violation exists elsewhere. (3) `ownVendorPENs` is CURATED with a mandatory reason per row and every PEN looked up in `testdata/iana/enterprise_numbers.tsv`, never derived from string similarity: six shipped pairs share no word with their slug and are all correct (`hpe_proliant_dl380`→232 Compaq, `dell_emc_unity`→1139 EMC, `nokia_7750_sr12`→6527, `netapp_ontap`→789 Network Appliance, `arista_7280r3`→30065, `ibm_power_s922`→2). (4) `arcPENOf` matches on a SUB-IDENTIFIER BOUNDARY: PEN 2 is a string prefix of 2011 (Huawei), 2620 (Check Point), 2636 (Juniper) and 25461 (Palo Alto), all of which ship, so a `HasPrefix` implementation reports four vendors as IBM — the `Contains "5703"` bug nl6#587's review found in `pollaris.mdx`. The two single-arc guards STAY: they say something this one does not, that those two specific arcs must not come back by name. **THE GUARD COVERS THREE SURFACES, and two of them held a live defect** — which is the argument for the general guard over a fifth single-arc one. `vendorOIDs` (`metrics_oids.go`) served `sonicwall_nsa6700` four OIDs under `1.3.6.1.4.1.8714`, **iNOC, Inc.**, where SonicWALL is **8741**: a digit transposition, never read by any test, answering live values through `getMetricValue` and enumerated into every walk by `GetSortedMetricOIDs`, while the same profile's resource files used 8741 throughout. `createDefaultResources` (`resources.go`) answered `sysObjectID.0` with `1.3.6.1.4.1.9.1.1`, ciscoSystems — a PRODUCTION path, so a device with no resource file identified itself as Cisco hardware; it now answers the documentation PEN, while `sysDescr` deliberately still reads "Cisco IOS Software" (a DisplayString a human reads, not an identity a collector keys on). **Neither fix moves a golden digest** — measured — because `collectShippedOIDs` walks resource files and both live in Go code, which is exactly why each needed a guard of its own: `TestEveryCodeServedVendorOIDIsItsOwnVendorArc` and `TestDefaultResourcesServeNoForeignVendorArc`, each with its own cross-profile positive control. **What the guard cannot see, stated rather than glossed.** In the VALUE position it sees `sysObjectID` and nothing else, which is narrower than "OID-typed values" sounds: the gate is the production predicate `snmpTypeTag(...) == ASN1_OBJECT_ID` and `oidTypeTable` has exactly ONE such row. `entPhysicalVendorType` is an OBJECT IDENTIFIER in RFC 4133 and ships enterprise-arc responses in six profiles that the main scan reads as OCTET STRINGs and skips — a coverage gap, not a live defect, closed separately and cheaply by `assertEntPhysicalVendorTypeIsNotCrossVendor` (224 enterprise-rooted values, none cross-vendor, of which **208 answer the synthetic `1.3.6.1.4.1.0.0`**; PEN 0 is `Reserved`, so those resolve to no vendor at all and the count is pinned as a known quantity rather than endorsed). Widening the predicate means adding an `oidTypeTable` row, which is a wire change. Also uncovered: whether the objects BELOW a correct PEN mean what the vendor's MIB says (nl6#569's audit of one profile found 8 of 11 wrong; all three `nvidia_*` profiles pass while every object under 5703 is invented); a MISSING arc, beyond the per-profile "serves at least one OID-typed value under its own PEN" census; and the trap catalogs' `snmpTrapEnterprise` values, gNMI and REST — the catalogs were audited by hand and are clean, and scanning them was considered and deliberately not added.
